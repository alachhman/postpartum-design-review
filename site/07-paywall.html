Screen 07 · plan activation

Paywall

Shown at the moment her plan is revealed, not on launch. Annual first, no weekly tier, and the free safety layer stated out loud on the screen that is asking for money.

$79.99/yr · $12.99/mo7-day trial, card requiredSafety never paywalled
The two constraints that shape this screenFirst: safety is never behind the paywall — logging, the check-in and floor warnings stay free forever, because that is what makes the app recommendable by a lactation consultant and what prevents a headline nobody recovers from. Second: the credential is the pricing lever. This audience decides whether to pay for nutrition advice based on who stands behind the number, and they have been burned badly enough that the reviewing clinicians need to be named before the ask, not after. §0A.4 · §brief 7

The arithmetic does the selling. $79.99 shown next to $6.67 a month, with the exact charge date and how to cancel. No timer, no crossed-out price, no “83% off”. §8.1

Annual first because of how this ends. Churn here is structural — users wean out in 12–18 months — so the annual plan is the honest way to capture the value, not a trick.

What is deliberately absent

Each of these is standard in the category and each one is a reason this audience says they pull back.

Common patternWhy it is not here
Countdown timer on the offerManufactured urgency, and the single most-named dark pattern in the review corpus.
Crossed-out “was $199”A price that was never charged. Trust is the whole product.
Weekly tier at $4.99Monetises confusion, churns 30–50% at first renewal, and is off-brand for a trust-led product.
Paywalled barcode scannerThe biggest single churn driver in this category. It stays free.
Free tier limited to N logs a dayWould make the free safety layer decorative, which defeats the point of having one.
Interstitial adsNo third-party advertising data sharing, ever. It is a stated differentiator, and reviewers describe ads appearing while they eat.
