Screen 04 · under ninety seconds

Onboarding

Eight screens, and the seventh is the one that matters. She sees her whole plan — targets, floor, and the arithmetic behind both — before she is asked for an account.

8 screens · ≤ 90 secondsValue before the account wallSix-week gate, no override
What the target-preview screen is actually forNot teaching her what a calorie floor is. That idea is already installed — women quote 1,500, 1,800 and 2,000 to each other unprompted, and even the most restriction-tolerant corners of the internet carve out an exception for nursing. Her question is narrower and more urgent: why is your number different from the one I read? So the screen shows the arithmetic and lets her audit it. §0A.1 · §2.3

The eight screens

Birth date first, because it gates everything. The gentle question about food sits at six, after she has already seen that this app asks about energy before it asks about weight.

Energy and strength come before weight. The body-neutral path is first in the list, not a toggle underneath it — the highest-voted motivation in the research is being able to pick the baby up a hundred times a day.

The gentle question has no score and no submit. It never says screening, and it is the one question she has no reason to lie about. §7

The six-week gate

Baby under six weeks. Maintenance only, no override, until she confirms she has had her postpartum check.

A · Ledgermaintenance only
10:05
Your baby is three weeks old
So I’m not going to give you a deficit yet. Until you’ve had your six-week check, this runs in maintenance only.
Eat about2,400
Never below1,800
Enough to heal on and keep your supply up.
Everything else works now — logging, the daily check-in, floor warnings. Weight targets switch on once you tell me you’ve been checked over.

It reads as a decision, not an error. No lock icon, no greyed-out UI, no “upgrade to unlock”. The app is choosing this. §brief 6
