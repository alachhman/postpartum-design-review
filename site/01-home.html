Supply status leads, calories follow — the positioning rendered on screen one. All three directions keep that order. They disagree about what the calorie number should be.

Sarah · 11 weeks postpartum · combo feeding Floor 1,800 · target 2,000 Supply steady, 5 days
The disagreement worth having The brief specifies “1,460 remaining.” The research argues that number imports the wrong instinct — MyFitnessPal taught 200 million people that remaining is a budget that should approach zero, and a floor is the opposite idea §2.1. A subtracts the floor out and shows what she still owes herself. B keeps the brief’s number and fixes only the geometry. C argues the daily number shouldn’t be the hero at all. Pick one before anything else gets built — every other screen inherits it.

The floor is a sector, not a tick. A hatched region the fill has to cross out of — a hairline radial mark invites knife-edge anxiety and cannot be labelled. §2.5

Safe is a band, not an edge. 1,800–2,300, borrowed from MacroFactor’s dead-band dial. There is no single line to sit exactly on.

Honest about the tension: this keeps the brief’s “1,460 left,” which is still drain-to-zero grammar wrapped around fill-to-safety geometry. That is the cost of staying faithful. §2.1

Supply-dip state

Late afternoon, two days after her check-ins moved below her own usual range. This is the entry point to the supply-protect nudge — it must read as observation, never verdict.

Copy constraint, non-negotiable KellyMom — the resource this audience already reads — states plainly that pump output is not a valid measure of supply. So the banner can say “two days below your usual” (a statement about her own reported trend) and can never say “your supply is low” (a clinical claim the app cannot substantiate). Every direction below obeys this. §1.1 · §3.1

The palette runs on a clock. Day → dusk → a 2–5am mode. Defensible on legibility grounds alone: what helps at night is reducing total emitted light, not shifting hue. Never market it as protecting sleep hormones — that claim has no evidence. §4.4 · §1.4

The bad days stay visible in context. Two misses sit inside 24 protected days. The month is the frame, so a bad day can’t become the story.

3:14 a.m.

The hour this product is actually opened. Dark mode is not a preference here, it is the primary condition — and the right design response is that the app asks for nothing.

What the evidence does and does not support Reducing total emitted light is supported. A red or amber colour shift doing anything on its own is not — the trials are null and individual light sensitivity varies about 58-fold between people. So build the dim mode and defend it on legibility, never on melatonin. The real failure mode in this category is dim-grey-on-black: every foreground here sits at 7:1 or better, with a 20pt type floor in the night palette. §1.4 · §4.4
